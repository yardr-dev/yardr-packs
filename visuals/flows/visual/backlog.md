summary: a visual bead waits here until its brief names the format, the size and what to say

A visual bead asks for three directions to pick from and hold, not for a page
to ship. Before it is sent to `directions` its body says:

- the format: what the visual is (a poster, a social card, a cover, a slide)
  and where it will be seen;
- the size: for screen, the exact pixels, width by height (1080x1350, say);
  for print, the trim size and which it is (A4, A5, US Letter, say);
- the subject: what the product, place or person is, in one concrete sentence;
- the audience: who sees the visual;
- the job: the one thing the visual gets its viewer to do or feel;
- the words: the copy that must appear, or that the designer writes it;
- what the brief fixes: brand colours, typefaces, a logo, pictures that must
  appear, or that nothing is fixed;
- reference work: pictures or addresses of work the person likes or dislikes,
  each with what to take from it or leave;
- what is not wanted: looks already tried and turned down.

Send it with `yardr bead advance <id> --to directions`.
